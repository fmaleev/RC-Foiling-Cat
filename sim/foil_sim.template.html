How this works, and how far to trust it

The page runs the same physics as the project's velocity prediction program (analysis/vpp.py). Hull wave resistance (Michell thin-ship integral with catamaran interference) is precomputed over boat speed and the weight the hulls carry, then interpolated. Everything else is computed live:

  • ITTC-57 friction
  • appendage drag
  • lifting-line foil lift and induced drag
  • the sail lift/drag model
  • the heel and bow-down limits

Take-off happens when both foil pairs can carry their share of the weight: the elevator's share is set by where the centre of gravity sits between the foils. With wand-driven flaps, the main foils can add up to 15 deg of flap at low speed, so they need less speed to lift their share. Without flaps the boat has no ride-height stability once it flies. The flap range also sets the speeds over which the flaps can hold ride height.

These are model predictions. Several inputs are assumptions rather than measurements:

  • the sail maximum lift coefficient (1.3)
  • the flap effectiveness
  • the 10 % added mass
  • scaling of the bow-down limit with weight

Foil lift at Reynolds numbers near 10^5 and close to the surface may be lower than predicted.
